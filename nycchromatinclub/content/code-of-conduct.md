title: Code of conduct
summary: What the club expects of everyone at its meetings.
---

The NYC Chromatin Club exists so that scientists across the city can talk to each other openly. That only works if everyone can take part without harassment or condescension — and if unpublished work can be discussed without fear of it leaking.

## What we expect

- **Treat everyone as a colleague.** Questions after a talk should be about the science. Hostile, dismissive, or grandstanding questioning is not acceptable, and it is especially damaging when directed at a student.
- **No harassment of any kind.** This includes offensive comments about gender, gender identity, sexual orientation, disability, physical appearance, race, religion, or nationality; unwelcome sexual attention; deliberate intimidation; and sustained disruption of talks.
- **Respect unpublished data.** Much of what is presented is unpublished. Do not photograph slides or posters, and do not share results outside the meeting, without the presenter's explicit permission.
- **Make room for early-career scientists.** The club is weighted toward students and postdocs by design. Senior scientists are asked to leave space in the question period rather than fill it.

## Reporting

If you experience or witness a problem at a club event, contact any member of the [steering committee](/about/#steering-committee) in person, or email <info@nycchromatinclub.org>. Reports will be handled discreetly.

The committee may ask anyone violating this code to leave an event, and may decline to admit them to future ones.
