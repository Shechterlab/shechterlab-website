      title: About the club
      text: |
        New York City has one of the densest concentrations of chromatin research anywhere — and for a long time those labs barely spoke to each other. A postdoc at Rockefeller could spend three years on a problem a group in the Bronx had already solved, and neither would know.

        The **NYC Chromatin Club** was founded in 2020 to fix that. It began as a virtual seminar series when the pandemic closed the campuses, and grew into a standing, city-wide community with an annual symposium, a shared Slack forum, and a mailing list that carries seminar announcements across every participating institution.

        The club is deliberately weighted toward **early-career scientists**. Graduate students, postdocs, and new investigators get the podium; the symposium program is built from submitted abstracts rather than an invitation list. There are no dues, no membership application, and no institutional requirement beyond working on chromatin somewhere in the New York area.

  - block: faq
    id: faq
    content:
      title: Common questions
      subtitle: ''
      items:
        - question: Who can join?
          answer: Anyone working on chromatin, epigenetics, or nuclear biology at a New York–area institution — faculty, postdocs, graduate students, staff scientists, technicians, and undergraduates. There is no application and no fee.
        - question: Do I have to be at one of the listed institutions?
          answer: No. The steering committee happens to be drawn from eight institutions, but the club is open to the whole New York area. If you are nearby and you work on chromatin, you are in scope.
        - question: How do I get a talk at the symposium?
          answer: Submit an abstract. Talks and posters are selected from submitted abstracts, with priority given to early-career scientists — there is no separate nomination process, and established investigators are not given automatic slots.
        - question: Does it cost anything?
          answer: No. The mailing list, the Slack forum, and the annual symposium are all free, including lunch at the symposium. Costs are covered by the host institution and by sponsors.
        - question: Can I attend remotely?
          answer: Seminar announcements on the mailing list include Zoom links where the host provides them. The annual symposium is an in-person event — the poster session and the hallway conversation are most of the point.
        - question: How can my company sponsor the symposium?
          answer: Sponsorship helps cover the venue, catering, and poster printing. Get in touch through the [Join](/join/) page and the committee will send the current sponsorship options.
