[Joseph DeAngelo](/authors/joseph-deangelo/), [Aliza Silverstein](/authors/aliza-silverstein/) and David Shechter have published [Protein arginine methylation rewires molecular interactions](/publications/arginine-methylation-rewires-interactions-2026/) in [*Cell Chemical Biology*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chembiol.2026.09.011) — and it is the **cover article** of the October 15 special issue on post-translational modifications.

The review proposes an interaction-equilibrium framework: because methylation preserves the positive charge of the guanidinium group while changing its hydrogen-bond topology and shape, it does not simply switch an interaction on or off. It redistributes binding capacity among competing protein, nucleic acid, and higher-order assembly states. That framing accommodates both reader-dependent and reader-independent effects of methylarginine, and it matters most in the intrinsically disordered, arginine-rich regions where multivalent interactions are decided.

The paper is open access.
