PRMT5 is overexpressed in many cancers and is the target of several clinical trials, but why the enzyme is essential to the cell has been unclear. [Jacob Roth](/authors/jacob-roth/) and our collaborative team have now identified a major part of the answer: cells need PRMT5 to make histones. The work is [published in *Nature Communications*](/publications/prmt5-histone-production-2025/).

Using time-resolved nascent transcriptional profiling, quantitative proteomics, and imaging, the paper shows that PRMT5 activity sustains histone transcription and histone protein synthesis through S phase. Inhibiting or depleting PRMT5 causes a rapid, p53-independent loss of histone mRNA, then of histone protein, and replication-associated nuclear abnormalities follow. Depleting HINFP, the histone gene transcription factor, produces the same genomic instability, which ties the defect specifically to histone production rather than to a general stress response. Histone H4 is present at histone genes and at histone locus bodies, where H4R3me2s is enriched — a model in which PRMT5-mediated H4 methylation regulates histone transcription.

A proliferating cell has to double its histones every cycle. Placing PRMT5 at the center of that supply gives a mechanistic reason for its essentiality, and a specific lens on what PRMT5 inhibitors are doing to dividing cells.
