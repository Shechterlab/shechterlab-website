[Jacob Roth](/authors/jacob-roth/) and colleagues' paper on [PRMT5-dependent histone production](/publications/prmt5-histone-production-2025/) is published in *Nature Communications*, after [provisional acceptance](/news/2026-08-roth-provisionally-accepted/) in August.

PRMT5 activity sustains histone transcription and histone protein synthesis during S phase. Inhibiting or knocking down PRMT5 depletes histone mRNA rapidly and independently of p53, costing the cell its histone proteins and leaving replication-associated nuclear abnormalities behind. Depleting the histone gene transcription factor HINFP does much the same, tying defective histone production to loss of genome integrity. Histone H4 accumulates at histone genes and at histone locus bodies, where H4R3me2s is enriched — placing PRMT5 at the center of histone homeostasis and giving a mechanistic reason for its essential role in proliferating cells.
