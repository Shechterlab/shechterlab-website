Proliferating cells must allocate finite methylation potential across DNA, RNA, protein, lipid, and small-molecule methylation while duplicating and packaging their genome. We are testing the hypothesis that this allocation follows a reproducible **methyl-spending hierarchy** that determines how cells respond to methyl-donor stress.

GNMT provides a tunable supply-side handle on the SAM:SAH ratio. PRMT5-H4 methylation provides a tractable demand-side readout for histone supply and chromatin assembly. PEMT and NNMT extend the model to membrane biogenesis and oncogenic methyl-donor sinks. Together these tools let us ask which methylation reactions fail first, how the failure propagates across DNA, RNA, protein, and lipid outputs, and whether distinct methyl-economy disruptions converge on shared cell-state failures.
