abstract: "Protein arginine methyltransferases (PRMTs) are required for the regulation of RNA processing factors. Type I PRMT enzymes catalyze mono- and asymmetric dimethylation; type II enzymes catalyze mono- and symmetric dimethylation. To resolve the specific mechanisms of PRMT activity in splicing regulation, we inhibited type I and type II PRMTs and probed their transcriptomic consequences. Using our SKaTER-seq (Splicing Kinetics and Transcript Elongation Rates by Sequencing) method, analysis of co-transcriptional splicing showed that PRMT inhibition altered splicing rates. Surprisingly, co-transcriptional splicing kinetics did not correlate with the final splicing changes seen in polyadenylated RNA, particularly for retained introns. Together, the data show that PRMTs regulate the post-transcriptional processing of nuclear detained introns through Sm and CHTOP arginine methylation, and that type I and type II PRMT activity have inverse effects on this process."
