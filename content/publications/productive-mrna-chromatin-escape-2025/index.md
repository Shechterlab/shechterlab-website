abstract: "Protein arginine methyltransferase 5 (PRMT5) catalyzes symmetric arginine dimethylation of RNA-binding proteins and influences RNA splicing and gene expression. A major function of PRMT5 is to promote chromatin escape of mRNAs, which we designate genomically retained incompletely processed polyadenylated transcripts (GRIPPs). Using nascent and spike-in normalized fractionated transcriptomics with proteomics, we find that PRMT5 inhibition in mammalian cells causes polyadenylated mRNA and Sm protein accumulation on chromatin, with these retained transcripts being intron-rich and slow to splice. PRMT5 inhibition and isogenic SNRPB mutants show that Sm tail methylation is essential to prevent RNA detention on chromatin. This work reveals how PRMT5-dependent Sm methylation regulates RNA homeostasis by ensuring that polyadenylated transcripts complete splicing and escape chromatin."
