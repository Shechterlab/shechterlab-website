---
title: 'NPM1-Mutant Acute Myeloid Leukemia'
date: 2026-06-28
summary: 'TTLL4-dependent glutamylation of NPM1c as a therapeutic target in NPM1-mutant AML.'
---

NPM1-mutant AML is driven by a cytoplasmic-relocalized form of the histone chaperone NPM1 (NPM1c). We have identified the **TTLL4 glutamyltransferase** as a therapeutic target in this disease, where altered glutamylation of NPM1c and related proteins reshapes chromatin association and oncogenic transcriptional programs.

This builds directly on our biochemical work on glutamylation of histone chaperones (see [Glutamylation, Histone Chaperones, and Disordered Regions →](/research/glutamylation-chaperones)) and is supported by collaborations with the Gritsman and Kitamura labs at Einstein.

**Questions we are pursuing:**

- How does TTLL4-dependent glutamylation of NPM1c change its chromatin association and downstream transcriptional program?
- Is TTLL4 activity pharmacologically tractable as a first-in-class NPM1c-mutant AML therapeutic?
- Which other glutamylation substrates contribute to the oncogenic phenotype?
