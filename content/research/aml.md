---
title: 'NPM1-Mutant Acute Myeloid Leukemia'
date: 2026-06-28
summary: 'TTLL4-dependent glutamylation of NPM1c as a therapeutic target in NPM1-mutant AML.'
---

NPM1-mutant AML, the most common genetic subtype of acute myeloid leukemia, is driven by a mutant form of the histone chaperone NPM1 that mislocalizes to the cytoplasm (NPM1c). We found that the **TTLL4 glutamyltransferase** modifies NPM1c and related proteins, changing how they associate with chromatin and rewiring the leukemic transcriptional program — which makes TTLL4 a therapeutic target we're now going after directly.

This grew out of our basic biochemistry on glutamylation and histone chaperones (see [Glutamylation, Histone Chaperones, and Disordered Regions →](/research/glutamylation-chaperones)), and we're pursuing it with the Gritsman and Kitamura labs at Einstein.

**Questions we are pursuing:**

- How does TTLL4-dependent glutamylation of NPM1c change its chromatin association and downstream transcriptional program?
- Is TTLL4 activity pharmacologically tractable as a first-in-class NPM1c-mutant AML therapeutic?
- Which other glutamylation substrates contribute to the oncogenic phenotype?
