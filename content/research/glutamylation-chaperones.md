---
title: 'Histone Chaperones, Glutamylation, and Intrinsically Disordered Regions'
date: 2026-06-28
summary: 'How acidic intrinsically disordered regions in histone chaperones are tuned by PTMs to encode DNA mimicry and chromatin assembly capacity.'
---

A major direction in the lab asks how post-translational modifications regulate the **acidic, intrinsically disordered regions** of nuclear proteins, particularly histone chaperones. These disordered acidic segments are central to histone binding, DNA mimicry, and chromatin assembly.

The lab has built a mechanistic framework for how phosphorylation, arginine methylation, and **glutamylation** tune histone chaperone behavior. We have shown that glutamylation of NPM2 and Nap1 acidic regions increases DNA mimicry and histone chaperone efficiency, and that intramolecular regulation of nucleoplasmin controls histone binding and release. We are now extending this logic to NPM1, PTMA, and related proteins, and to the **TTLL4 glutamyltransferase** that establishes many of these modifications.

This work also provides the chromatin-assembly side of our broader picture of the cellular **methyl economy**: histone chaperones gate how methylated histones move from synthesis to chromatin assembly, and the same disordered acidic regions are themselves substrates for the small handful of enzymes that write and erase these PTMs.

**Questions we are pursuing:**

- How does glutamylation modulate the conformational ensembles and binding surfaces of histone chaperones?
- Which enzymes establish and remove functionally important glutamylation states, and how is the writer-eraser balance regulated?
- How general is this logic across acidic, disordered nuclear proteins?
